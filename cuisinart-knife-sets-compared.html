Cuisinart Sells Four Very Different Knife Sets.

Eighteen dollars buys a 12-piece color-coded starter set. Eighty dollars buys a 15-piece hollow-handle block. In between sits a 10-piece ColorCore set with blade guards and a 7-piece block with a sharpener built into the slot — and the piece count does not climb in a straight line with the price. We compared the published listing details and current Amazon prices for all four to work out which tier actually matches which kitchen.

The four sets

One brand, four spec sheets, a $61 spread

Cuisinart runs four separate knife sets on Amazon, and the prices land at $17.99, $24.95, $67.96 and $80.04. That is not a smooth, evenly spaced ladder — a $7 gap between the cheapest two, then a $43 jump to the third, then another $11 to the fourth. Piece counts don't track price either: 12 pieces at $17.99, 10 at $24.95, 7 at $67.96, 15 at $80.04. The most expensive set has the most pieces; the second-most-expensive has the fewest. Piece count alone tells you almost nothing about where a Cuisinart set sits on this list.

What actually separates the four, once you read past the headline numbers, is construction and what's built into the block, not raw knife count. The entry set is basic cutlery with multicolor handles and no stated block. ColorCore adds color-coded rivets and guards for a little more. The Essentials set drops to 7 pieces but adds a block with a sharpener built into one slot. The top set adds hollow handle construction and the largest piece count. Four different reasons to spend four different amounts under one brand name. The rest of this guide works through each tier cheapest to priciest, then ends with picks sorted by how many blades you actually reach for.

What's consistent across all four

Cuisinart, stainless steel, one brand name on every box

All four sets are sold under the Cuisinart name and all four describe stainless steel blades. Beyond that, each listing states its own construction details separately — none of the four titles cross-reference another tier, so nothing about the cheaper sets can be assumed to carry over to the pricier ones, or the other way around.

What changes between tiers

Block presence, sharpener, rivet color, handle construction

The headline differences are whether a block and sharpener are included (only the $67.96 set states both), whether the rivets or handles carry color (only the $17.99 and $24.95 sets), and whether the handle is a riveted scale or a hollow stamped tube (only the $80.04 set states hollow handle construction). Piece count moves unevenly — 12, 10, 7, 15 — so it is the least useful number for comparing the four.

The cheapest tier

The $18 entry set: what it skips

The Cuisinart C55-01-12PCKS Collection is a 12-piece multicolor set Cuisinart markets under its Advantage Cutlery Set line, priced at $17.99, and its own listing states one spec with any precision: an 8-inch size. Beyond that figure and the piece count, the title tells you what the set looks like — multicolored — more than what it's made of. It does not state a knife block, a built-in sharpener, forged or stamped construction, or a full-tang claim, and by this site's rule we don't assert a spec a listing doesn't give. That isn't a knock on the set; a dozen color-coded blades for under $20 is a complete, cheap way into a kitchen. It's a reason to read the listing yourself before assuming it includes storage or maintenance features the pricier Cuisinart tiers state outright.

The "multicolor" detail is worth a second look rather than dismissing as pure decoration. Color-coded knife handles are a common device in this category generally, sometimes used to assign a color per food type as a visual reminder to avoid cross-contamination between raw meat and produce. Cuisinart's own listing for this set doesn't publish a suggested color-to-food pairing, so we won't claim that's the intended system here — only that color-coded handles are the kind of feature that can serve that purpose if a household chooses to use them that way.

What the $18 price buys you, stated plainly, is 12 pieces of stainless cutlery with an 8-inch reference size and color-differentiated handles. What it skips, by omission rather than by stated fact, is everything the other three Cuisinart tiers lead with: a block, a sharpener, blade guards, and any construction claim beyond "stainless steel." If any of those four things is the reason you're shopping, this is not the tier that states having them.

The second tier

ColorCore at $24.95: fewer pieces, more identification

The Cuisinart C77CR-10P ColorCore™ set costs $24.95 — seven dollars more than the entry set — and ships with 10 pieces plus blade guards, two pieces fewer than the $17.99 set, not more. Shopping by piece count alone, stepping up to ColorCore looks like a downgrade. What it adds instead is color assigned per blade through rivets rather than full handles, and a guard for each knife, which the entry set's listing doesn't mention. Cuisinart's own listing states one blade-length figure for this set: 3.9 inches, noticeably shorter than the entry set's published 8-inch reference, suggesting a different mix of knife sizes even though neither listing itemizes its contents knife by knife.

The blade guards are worth not skipping past. A guard is a hard sheath that slides over an individual blade in storage, protecting the edge and whoever reaches into the drawer next — a different job from a sharpener, which maintains the edge rather than protecting it, and different again from a block, which stores knives upright on the counter rather than flat in a drawer. Our guide to the extra pieces hiding in a knife block set covers how guards, shears and sharpening slots each count as a "piece" without doing the same job — exactly the trap a side-by-side piece count sets for this ColorCore tier.

"ColorCore" as a name implies the color runs through the handle's core rather than sitting on the surface, but the listing title specifically says "Color Rivet," which describes the rivet, not the whole handle. We won't stretch the branding further than the stated spec: the confirmed detail is color-coded rivets, not a claim about the handle material beneath them. If the construction of the handle itself — what it's made of, how it's reinforced — is the deciding factor for you, our guide to knife handle materials covers what terms like this typically mean and what to check on a specific listing before buying.

The third tier

The 7-piece Essentials block and its built-in sharpener

The Cuisinart Stainless Steel Essentials Cutlery Block Set costs $67.96 and drops to just 7 pieces — the fewest of any tier here — while being the first Cuisinart set on this list to state both a cutlery block and a sharpener built into one of its slots. That's a $43 jump from the ColorCore tier for three fewer pieces; the honest read is that the money is paying for the block and the sharpening mechanism, not additional blades. Seven matched knives stored upright in a block, with a slot that touches up the edge each time a knife is drawn out or put back, is a different proposition from a dozen loose pieces in a drawer.

A built-in sharpener works the same way across this whole category: the blade passes through a fixed abrasive element in the slot, which keeps a working edge from going fully dull between proper sharpenings, without replacing a whetstone or resetting a damaged edge. It's a real convenience for anyone who would otherwise never sharpen a knife at all, and close to irrelevant if you already maintain your knives by hand. Our guide to pull-through sharpeners versus honing steels breaks down exactly what a slot sharpener does and doesn't do to an edge, which is worth reading before paying the premium this tier commands specifically for that feature.

The listing does not publish a Rockwell hardness figure or a forged-versus-stamped claim, so we treat "stainless steel" here the way we'd treat it on any listing that stops at the material name: real and common, one detail short of a number you could compare against another brand's published hardness. Seven pieces at $67.96 works out to roughly $9.70 per piece before weighting in the block and sharpener themselves — worth holding against the $17.99 set's roughly $1.50 per piece, two very different products under one brand name.

The top tier

Hollow handle construction on the $79 set, and what it changes

The Cuisinart C77SS-15PK is the most expensive and the largest set on this list: $80.04 for 15 pieces, and the only one of the four whose title states hollow handle construction. A hollow handle is built differently from the riveted-scale handles on most of this site's other picks. Instead of attaching a separate wood, plastic or composite grip to a tang with rivets, the same sheet of stamped stainless steel that forms the blade is bent and welded into a hollow tube that becomes the handle, usually with a filling inside to add balancing weight. There is no separate handle material riveted onto the steel to crack, swell, or work loose over years of washing, because the blade and the handle's outer shell are one continuous piece of metal.

That construction is a trade-off, not a pure upgrade. Because the handle is typically formed from stamped sheet rather than built up from a forged bolster, a hollow handle knife is usually lighter in the hand than a comparably sized forged knife with a riveted handle, even though it can look chunkier. It also can't be re-gripped or customized the way a riveted handle with removable scales sometimes can, since handle and blade are one welded piece. Neither is a defect — plenty of cooks prefer the lighter, seamless feel, and a welded handle has nothing to loosen. It's a different answer to the same question the ColorCore rivets and the Essentials block's riveted handles answer differently: how the handle attaches to the steel. Our knife handle materials guide covers riveted construction in more depth for comparison.

At 15 pieces, this is also the largest Cuisinart set here, which combined with the stated hollow handle construction is likely where most of the $11 gap above the Essentials block's $67.96 goes — more pieces and a more labor-intensive handle, not a sharpener or block feature the listing doesn't call out. The title doesn't state a built-in sharpener, so don't assume it carries over from the Essentials tier; check the live listing before buying.

FAQ

Common questions

What does the $18 Cuisinart set actually skip compared to the pricier tiers?

Its listing doesn't state a knife block, a built-in sharpener, blade guards, or a forged-versus-stamped construction claim — the only specs it commits to are the 12-piece count, multicolor handles, and an 8-inch reference size. That's not proof those features are absent, only that the listing doesn't confirm them, so we don't assume they carry over from the three pricier Cuisinart tiers that do state them.

Why does the $24.95 ColorCore set have fewer pieces than the $17.99 set?

Price and piece count don't move together across these four Cuisinart tiers. The ColorCore set's extra seven dollars buys color-coded rivets per blade and included blade guards, not additional knives — it ships with 10 pieces against the entry set's 12. If you're comparing Cuisinart tiers on piece count alone, this is the pair where that comparison actively misleads.

Is the built-in sharpener on the Essentials block as good as sharpening by hand?

No, and it isn't trying to be. A slot sharpener passes the blade through a fixed abrasive element each time you draw it out or put it back, which keeps a working edge from going fully dull between proper sharpenings. It can't reset a damaged edge or change the blade's angle the way a whetstone or honing steel can. Our guide to pull-through sharpeners versus honing steels covers the difference in more depth.

What is hollow handle construction, and does it matter which Cuisinart tier has it?

A hollow handle is formed when the same sheet of stamped steel that makes the blade is bent and welded into a hollow tube that becomes the handle, usually with a filling inside for balance, rather than attaching a separate riveted scale. Only the $80.04 C77SS-15PK states this construction among the four Cuisinart sets here. It means there's no separate handle material to loosen or crack, but the handle also can't be re-gripped the way a riveted scale sometimes can.
