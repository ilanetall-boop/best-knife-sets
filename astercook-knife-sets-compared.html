Astercook Sells Three Knife Sets. Here’s What Separates Them.

Same brand, three spec sheets, a $41 gap between the cheapest and the most expensive. One set leans on an anti-rust coating, two call themselves German steel, and only one of those two states a built-in sharpener. We compared the published listing details and current Amazon prices for all three to work out what the money actually buys as you move up the line.

The three sets

Same brand, three different spec sheets

Astercook runs three separate knife sets on Amazon at three separate price points, and the gaps between them are not small. The 13-Piece set with an anti-rust coating lists at $23.98. Step up to the 15-piece German-steel set that includes a block and a built-in sharpener and the price is $59.99 — sixteen dollars more. Step up again to the 15-Piece German Steel Knife Block Set and the price is $64.98, a further twenty-five dollars, and forty-one dollars above where you started. Three sets, one brand, and the difference between the cheapest and the most expensive is more than double.

What makes this worth comparing rather than just picking the middle option is that the price curve doesn't move in a straight line with the features. The cheapest set is the only one that doesn't claim German steel. The middle set is the only one of the two German-steel sets whose listing states a sharpener. The most expensive set, despite costing the most, does not make that same sharpener claim in its title. The obvious assumption — that each step up buys a strictly better version of the last — doesn't hold cleanly here. The rest of this guide walks through what each listing actually states, so you can match the tier to what you need rather than to where it sits in the price order.

What's consistent across all three

Astercook, stainless steel, dishwasher-safe claims

All three sets are sold under the Astercook name and all three are built around stainless steel blades. The 13-piece set states dishwasher-safe directly in its title; the two German-steel sets don't repeat that claim in their titles, so if dishwasher safety specifically matters to you on either of those, confirm it on the listing rather than assuming it carries over from the cheaper set.

What changes between tiers

Coating, steel origin claim, sharpener, and price

The headline differences are the anti-rust coating on the cheapest set versus the German-steel claim on the other two, the presence of a stated sharpener on the $59.99 set only, and a $41 spread across the line. Piece count moves only slightly — 13 versus 15 twice — so it is not the main thing you are paying for as you move up.

What the blade is made of

Anti-rust coating versus German stainless steel

The entry set's full title is specific about one thing: a "Healthy Anti-Rust Coating" over stainless steel blades. A coating sits on top of the metal underneath it; it is a surface treatment, not a different grade of steel. That's a reasonable way to fight rust spotting on a budget blade, and dishwasher-safe stainless at $23.98 for 13 pieces with six blade guards is a complete, honestly specified starter set on its own terms. What the listing does not state is a steel grade, a hardness figure, or a country of manufacture for the steel itself — "healthy anti-rust coating" describes a finish, not a material, and we won't infer a steel spec the listing doesn't give.

Both of the pricier sets instead lead with "German" in their titles — the $59.99 set as "15pcs German" and the $64.98 set as "German Steel." Neither title goes further than that single word to specify an alloy designation or a Rockwell hardness number, so "German steel" here functions the way it does across most of this category: a claim about manufacturing heritage rather than a lab-verifiable spec you can check against a certificate. It's real, common shorthand in this space, and also one detail short — a hardness figure — of what would let you compare it head-to-head against a competing brand's steel. If that distinction matters, our guide to what "high carbon stainless steel" actually guarantees walks through which steel claims are checkable and which are adjectives dressed as specifications; the same logic applies to a country-of-origin claim like "German."

Practically, the honest way to frame the top two tiers is not "cheap coated steel versus premium German steel," but "a coating claim at the bottom versus a heritage claim at the top, with no published hardness number differentiating any of the three." That doesn't make the anti-rust set worse or the German-labeled sets better by default — it means the steel-quality question isn't one these listings answer, regardless of price. The one feature called out by name

What the built-in sharpener adds to the price

Of the three listings, only one states a sharpener outright: the $59.99 "Knife Set for Kitchen with Block and Sharpener." The title puts the sharpener on equal footing with the block itself, which is a reasonable signal that Astercook considers it a selling point worth naming rather than a minor accessory buried in the bullet points. A sharpener built into the block slot works the same way across this category generally — the blade passes through a fixed abrasive element each time you draw it out or put it away, which keeps a working edge from going fully dull between proper sharpenings. It is not a substitute for a whetstone and won't fix a damaged edge, but it is a real convenience for anyone who would otherwise never sharpen anything.

The $23.98 entry set does not state a sharpener; its title calls out six blade guards instead, which is a different kind of accessory — guards protect the edge and your hand in storage or a drawer, they don't maintain sharpness over time. The $64.98 set's title, "Astercook Knife Set 15 Piece German Steel Kitchen Knife Block Set," doesn't mention a sharpener either. That's the detail most worth sitting with here: the most expensive of the three sets is the one whose title makes no sharpener claim, while the cheaper of the two German-steel sets does.

We won't guess at why. It's possible the $64.98 set includes a sharpening slot that simply isn't called out in the title. It's equally possible the extra $25 is paying for something else entirely — a different handle material, a heavier bolster — that isn't itemized in the data we have. If a built-in sharpener specifically is the deciding feature for you, confirm it directly on the $64.98 listing before buying on the assumption that "German steel" at a higher price automatically includes everything the cheaper German-steel set does.

What holds the knives

Block size and piece count across the three

Piece count is close across the line — 13 pieces at the bottom, 15 pieces on each of the two pricier sets — so it isn't where most of the $41 spread goes. What differs more is how the knives are stored. The $23.98 set's title specifies "6 Blade Guards" and makes no mention of a block; guards are individual sheaths that slide over each blade, which means this is most likely a blockless set you store in a drawer rather than a countertop set with knives standing upright in slots. Neither of the pricier sets mentions blade guards; both use the word "Block" directly in their titles — "with Block and Sharpener" on the $59.99 set, and "Knife Block Set" on the $64.98 set.

That's a real structural difference, not just a wording one. A block holds knives vertically or at a slant in individual slots and sits out on the counter as a visible piece of kitchen equipment. Blade guards do the edge-protection job without the counter footprint, which matters if space is tight; our guide on the best knife sets for RVs, campers and small kitchens covers when a guard-based set beats a block. Neither listing states the block's dimensions or material, so check the product photos on the specific $59.99 or $64.98 listing before buying if footprint matters.

"Block" on its own also doesn't say whether the slots are cut to a fixed size per knife or use a flexible insert that fits a range of blade widths — a distinction we cover in universal knife blocks versus fixed-slot blocks. Astercook's listings don't specify which design either block uses, so confirm that on the live page too. Where quality control differences actually show up

This site doesn't carry verified star ratings or review counts for these three listings, and our policy is not to paraphrase review sentiment we haven't verified — so we won't tell you what Astercook buyers supposedly say, because we don't have that data and inventing it would be no more honest than inventing a rating. What we can tell you, as a general fact about this category rather than a specific claim about Astercook, is where quality-control variance tends to live in a multi-tier line from one manufacturer: usually in the details a title doesn't mention at all. A brand that calls out "anti-rust coating" on its cheapest set and "German steel" on its pricier ones is telling you what it wants noticed first; edge consistency between knives, how securely the handle is riveted, and how tightly the block's slots are cut are the kind of details manufacturers across this whole price category rarely specify in a title at any tier.

None of the three listings publish a Rockwell hardness figure or alloy designation for any tier, so there's no number here to compare against a competing brand's published spec, the way our steel-claims guide recommends when a hardness figure exists. What's the real difference between the anti-rust 13-piece set and the two German-steel sets?

The 13-piece set's listing leads with a "Healthy Anti-Rust Coating" over stainless steel, which is a surface treatment rather than a steel grade. The two pricier sets lead with "German" instead, a heritage and manufacturing claim rather than a published hardness number. None of the three listings state a Rockwell figure or an alloy designation, so there's no lab-verifiable spec to compare between them — the real differences you can confirm are the coating claim itself, the German claim itself, the sharpener (stated only on the $59.99 set), and storage format (guards versus block).

Does the cheaper 15-piece German set's sharpener make it better than the pricier 15-piece set?

It makes it the only one of the two German-steel sets whose title states a built-in sharpener outright. Whether that makes it "better" depends on whether a sharpener is the feature you actually want. The $64.98 set doesn't repeat the sharpener line in its title, which could mean it doesn't include one, or could mean Astercook simply led with other details for that listing. We won't guess which; if the sharpener is the deciding factor, confirm it directly on the $64.98 listing before paying the premium.

Why does the 15-piece set without a stated sharpener cost more than the one with a sharpener?

We don't know, and the listing data we have doesn't say. The extra $25 could be going toward a detail that isn't itemized in either title — a heavier block, different handle material, additional hardware — or the $64.98 listing may simply not call out a sharpener that it does include. Piece count is identical at 15 for both, so piece count isn't the answer. This is exactly the kind of gap worth checking on the live Amazon listing rather than assuming price alone tells you what's inside the box.

Which Astercook tier should I buy for a small kitchen or a first apartment?
