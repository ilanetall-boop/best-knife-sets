One NeverDull Mechanism, Three Block Sizes.

Ninja sells its Foodi NeverDull knife system in three block sizes, and for once, more pieces does not mean a steadily higher price. The 12-piece set runs $149.99. The 17-piece set, with five more pieces, costs just $10 more at $229.99. The 14-piece set, sitting between those two piece counts, costs $179.99 — $140 more than the 12-piece set and $130 more than the 17-piece set, despite having fewer total pieces than one of them. Every tier carries the identical built-in sharpening slot, the entire reason anyone buys into this line. We compared what each listing actually states to work out what the piece count, the price and the mechanism itself are each buying you.

The three sets

One sharpening mechanism, three block sizes, and a price that doesn't climb in order

Ninja's Foodi NeverDull line comes in three sizes sold on Amazon: 12 pieces for $149.99, 14 pieces for $179.99, and 17 pieces for $229.99. Compare that to a line like Henckels Statement, where price climbs in a straight line with piece count, and the NeverDull lineup looks like a different animal. Going from 12 to 14 pieces adds just two pieces and costs $140.00 more. Going from 14 to 17 pieces adds three more pieces and the price drops $130.00. End to end, the 17-piece set — the largest of the three — costs only $10.00 more than the smallest, 12-piece set. The 14-piece tier is the outlier that breaks the pattern, not a sensible midpoint between the other two.

What doesn't change across any of the three is the thing the name advertises: a sharpening slot built into the block itself, so the knife is drawn through it on the way back in and the edge gets touched up every time. That mechanism, branded NeverDull, is the reason this line exists. The piece count and the price are where the three tiers actually diverge, and that's what the rest of this guide works through, tier by tier.

What's identical across all three

The built-in NeverDull sharpening slot

All three listings describe the same mechanism: a sharpener built into the knife block, drawn across the blade automatically as the knife is stored. None claims a sharper or more precise version at a higher price — the feature itself does not appear to change tier to tier, only how many knives come with it.

What actually changes tier to tier

Piece count, price, naming, and one steel claim

Piece count and price move in a genuinely odd order: 12/$149.99, 14/$179.99, 17/$229.99. The 12-piece and 14-piece listings both state “German Stainless Steel” blades; the 17-piece title doesn't repeat that wording. The 14-piece listing is also the only one called a “Knife System” rather than a “Knife Block Set” — worth noting, not assuming away.

The smallest tier

The 12-piece set: what it is, and what it leaves out

The Ninja K32012 Foodi NeverDull Premium Knife System, a 12-piece knife block set, is the cheapest entry point into the line at $149.99. Its listing title states German stainless steel knives and the built-in sharpener, and names its color as black. It does not itemize the twelve pieces beyond the total count, so we can't say how many are knives you'll use weekly versus a sharpening steel you'll barely touch — that breakdown isn't in the spec we have, and we won't guess at it.

What a 12-piece count reliably buys is the lowest entry price into a mechanism that, across all three tiers, appears identical: the same NeverDull slot built into the block. If the knives you'd actually reach for — a chef's knife, a paring knife, something serrated — fit inside twelve pieces, this tier gets you the sharpening slot for $140.00 less than the 14-piece set and only $10.00 less than the 17-piece set, which has five more pieces in the box.

That last point is worth sitting with before buying the smallest tier on price alone: at $149.99 you are ten dollars away from a seventeen-piece set with the same mechanism and the same stated black finish. The 12-piece set makes sense for a kitchen that wants the fewest pieces on the counter and the smallest block footprint — not simply for being the cheapest number on the page, because it very nearly isn't.

The outlier tier

The 14-piece set: a strange jump in price for two more pieces

The Ninja® NeverDull™ 14-Pc Knife System, model K32014, costs $179.99 — $140.00 more than the 12-piece set for just two additional pieces, and $130.00 more than the 17-piece set despite having three fewer pieces than it. Divided out, that two-piece jump from the 12-piece tier works out to $70.00 per added piece, a marginal rate wildly out of line with anything else in this line. By comparison, going from the 14-piece set up to the 17-piece set doesn't cost anything extra at all — it saves $130.00 while adding three more pieces. There is no reading of the piece-count math that makes the 14-piece tier look like a reasonable middle step between the other two.

The listing itself does carry more stated language than the other two: “Stays razor-sharp 10+ years. German stainless steel blades. A few swipes of the foolproof sharpener restores a superior edge.” That's a specific durability claim — a ten-year-plus edge life — that neither the 12-piece nor the 17-piece listing makes in the text we have. We can't verify a decade-long claim ourselves; nobody keeps a knife block in use for ten years before publishing a review of it. What we can say is the claim is stated for this tier specifically, specific enough to read less like boilerplate and more like a genuine distinction, whatever's actually driving the price difference.

One more detail worth flagging: Ninja calls this one a “Knife System,” not a “Knife Block Set” the way it names the 12-piece and 17-piece listings. A different product name on the same brand and the same core mechanism is a reasonable signal that this isn't simply “the 12-piece set with two more knives” — it may be separate packaging or a different hardware generation around the same sharpening slot. We can't confirm that from the spec alone. But it's a more plausible explanation than assuming Ninja charges $70 per knife for two extra pieces, and it's the honest reason we can't tell you with certainty what the extra $140 buys.

The largest tier

The 17-piece set, and where the extra knives go

The Ninja® NeverDull™ 17-Pc Knife Block Set, model K32017, costs $229.99 — only $10.00 more than the 12-piece set for five additional pieces, and $130.00 less than the 14-piece set despite having three more pieces than it. Its listing title states the built-in sharpener and the black finish, same as the other two, but it does not use the words “German Stainless Steel” the way the 12-piece and 14-piece titles do. We take listing silence as silence, not a denial: the absence of that wording doesn't mean the steel is different, only that this listing doesn't state it. If that phrase matters to your decision, check the current listing's bullet points and images before buying.

Neither this listing nor the 12-piece one breaks down what the extra five pieces actually are, so we won't guess at the knife-by-knife makeup. What we can say with confidence is the arithmetic: five more pieces for ten extra dollars is roughly $2.00 per added piece — the cheapest marginal rate of any step in this line, by a wide margin. If more blades around an identical sharpening slot is the goal, the 17-piece set meets it most cheaply.

The real question

Is the NeverDull slot worth paying extra for, across three different prices?

Here's the detail that makes this line genuinely confusing to shop: the built-in sharpening slot, the single feature that defines the NeverDull name, does not appear to get better, faster or more precise as the price climbs from $149.99 to $179.99. Nothing in any of the three listings claims an upgraded version of the mechanism at the higher price points. You are paying, in effect, for the same slot in three different boxes — and what separates those boxes is piece count, stated steel wording and, in the 14-piece's case, a decade-long durability claim nobody else in the line repeats.

That reframes the real decision. It isn't “how much sharpener do I want to buy” — every tier gets the identical mechanism. It's “how many knives do I want around that mechanism, and does one tier's stated claim change the calculation.” Judged purely on the slot itself, the cheapest way in is the 12-piece set at $149.99. Judged on pieces per dollar, the 17-piece set at $229.99 wins clearly. The 14-piece set only earns its price if the ten-year edge-life claim, or the different “Knife System” packaging behind it, genuinely matters more to you than either of those two comparisons.

Our guide on whether knife sets actually come with a warranty that means something is worth reading alongside the 14-piece set's “10+ years” line: a stated edge-life claim in marketing copy is not a warranty term you can act on if the knife doesn't hold up.

The math

Price per piece across the three tiers, spelled out

Divide total price by piece count and the gap becomes even starker than the sticker prices alone suggest. The 12-piece set runs about $18.33 per piece ($149.99 ÷ 12). The 17-piece set runs about $13.53 per piece ($229.99 ÷ 17) — the lowest of the three by a comfortable margin. The 14-piece set runs about $25.71 per piece ($179.99 ÷ 14), nearly double the 17-piece rate and well above the 12-piece average, despite sitting in the middle of the line by piece count alone.

Put another way: the 14-piece set is simultaneously the most expensive tier in total dollars, the most expensive per piece, and second-fewest in total pieces. It loses on every axis except the one its own listing leans on hardest — the ten-year edge-life claim and the different “Knife System” name. That's not a coincidence worth ignoring; it's the reason this comparison exists.

Setting the two lines side by side is a reminder that “more pieces costs more money, in order” is worth checking on every listing, not assumed from one brand to the next.

What's the real difference between the Ninja NeverDull 12-piece and 14-piece sets?

$140.00 more for just two additional pieces, which works out to about $70.00 per added piece — far above any other marginal rate in this line. Why does the 14-piece Ninja NeverDull set cost more than the 17-piece set?

We can't say for certain. The 14-piece listing uses a different product name, “Knife System” instead of “Knife Block Set,” and states a durability claim — “stays razor-sharp 10+ years” — the 17-piece listing doesn't repeat. That's a more plausible explanation than assuming Ninja simply charges more per knife, but it isn't confirmed, and the 17-piece set gets three more pieces for $130.00 less regardless.

Not based on what the three listings state. All three describe the same built-in sharpening slot, and none claims an upgraded or faster version at a higher price. The price differences appear to track piece count and stated claims, not the sharpener itself.
